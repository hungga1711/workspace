---
title: "Shadow AI in a small business"
description: "Your staff already use ChatGPT and Claude on their own accounts. How to find out where, what it puts at risk, and how to turn it into something you can measure in a week."
---

"Shadow AI" is AI your company did not choose: staff pasting work into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini on personal
accounts. In a 20–200 person company it is usually not rebellion. People found a tool that saves them an hour and
nobody told them what is allowed.

Banning it rarely works. The tools are one browser tab away, and the people using them are often your best people.
What works is the same thing that works for any other tool: know who uses it for what, set a few clear rules, and
check whether it actually helps.

## What it puts at risk

- **Customer data.** Contact details, ID numbers, contracts and prices pasted into a free plan may be used to train
  the provider's models, and leave no record on your side.
- **Quality.** AI answers are fluent and sometimes wrong. Without a record, nobody can tell which deliverable was
  checked and which was not.
- **Money.** Five people paying for their own subscriptions, three tools doing the same job, and no way to tell
  whether any of it shortens the work.

## Find out where AI is used, in one week

1. **Ask, without blame.** A two-question form: which AI tools do you use for work, and for what? You will get honest
   answers if the message is "we want to support this", not "we caught you".
2. **Look at what you already have.** ChatGPT Team, Claude Team and Microsoft 365 admin consoles list who uses them.
   Workspace can [import that history](/docs/ai-insights) so it counts from day one.
3. **Measure it from now on.** The Workspace [browser extension](/docs/ai-insights/extension) records which AI site,
   when and the topic, only after each person connects it, with a notice on every page. Staff see exactly what is
   recorded about them.

## Turn it into rules people follow

Write one page: approved tools, what never goes into any AI, and who to ask. Start from our
[AI usage policy template](/docs/guides/ai-usage-policy-template). Then turn on the sensitive-data net: Workspace can
warn, hide or block card numbers, ID numbers and other patterns before a prompt is sent
([how it works](/docs/guides/redact-customer-data-chatgpt)).

## Check that it helps

The question that matters is not "how many prompts" but "does work get done faster or better". Workspace links AI
conversations to tasks (when a prompt mentions a task code like T-157, it offers the link) and compares tasks done
with and without AI: time to done and on-time rate, with sample sizes, and no number until there is enough data.
See [How to measure AI ROI at work](/docs/guides/measure-ai-roi).
